# Close reading of the worldview

## 1. “The following statements summarize my current worldview”

This opening is modest and important. The page does not present itself as a completed scientific theory or universally binding revelation. It explicitly identifies the text as a current personal worldview.

The word `current` also makes the page revision-compatible. A timeless ontology is being articulated through a historically changing document. That is not necessarily a contradiction: the proposed metaphysical object may be static while karbytes’s phenomenal representation of it develops over time.

## 2. The static block multiverse

The first proposition says that the most macroscopic or “zoomed-out” level of existence is:

* Static.

* Unchanging.

* Timelessly eternal.

* Without temporal beginning.

* Without temporal end.

* Inclusive of every superposition of every phenomenon.

The phrase `timelessly eternal` contains a productive semantic tension. “Eternal” often means existing through unlimited time, while “timeless” means not existing temporally at all. The parenthetical definition resolves this partly by treating eternity as boundarylessness rather than duration. The totality does not survive from one moment to the next; it is simply not located within a larger temporal sequence.

This makes the block multiverse less like an infinitely old object and more like a complete mathematical structure. Nothing is added to it, removed from it, or altered inside it from an external viewpoint, because there is no external time against which such change could occur.

The claim that it contains “every superposition of every phenomenon” introduces modal plenitude: the totality does not merely contain the one history we experience. It contains an abstract domain encompassing alternatives.

What remains unspecified is whether those alternatives are:

* All physically possible states.

* All logically coherent states.

* All mathematically representable states.

* Every quantum-mechanical superposition allowed by a particular Hilbert space.

* Complete universe histories.

* Incomplete state fragments.

* Merely represented possibilities rather than equally actual realities.

That distinction matters because “everything possible” requires a definition of possibility.

## 3. Phenomena and noumena

The second proposition states:

> Every phenomenon has a noumenal counterpart but not every noumenon necessarily has a phenomenal counterpart.

This establishes an asymmetric relation.

Let (P) be the phenomenal domain and (N) the noumenal domain. The page implies a mapping:

[preformatted_text]
[
c:P → N
]

[end preformatted_text]

such that each rendered phenomenon has a counterpart in the abstract domain, while:

[preformatted_text]

[
N\setminus c(P)\neq\varnothing
]

[end preformatted_text]

may contain noumena which are never rendered.

This makes the noumenal field larger than the actually experienced world. Every experience is grounded in possibility, but not every possibility becomes an experience.

The use of `noumenon` is distinct from its traditional Kantian meaning. Here it is not simply an unknowable thing-in-itself standing behind appearance. It is redefined as a structured multiplicity of configurations associated with a possible phenomenon. The page is constructing a specifically KARBYTES ontology using inherited philosophical vocabulary.

## 4. The definition of phenomenon

A phenomenon is defined as:

* A finite allocation of space.

* A finite allocation of time.

* A finite allocation of energy.

* Sometimes an allocation of matter.

* Rendered by exactly one frame of reference.

* Distinguishable from other phenomena.

* Distinguishable from the invisible field of noumena.

The emphasis on finitude is central. A phenomenon is not merely “whatever appears”; it is a resource-bounded rendering. This resembles computation:

* A display has finite pixels.

* A processor has finite memory.

* An observation has finite resolution.

* An embodied agent has a finite sensory field.

* A webpage has finite rendered content.

* A frame contains only a limited portion of an underlying state space.

Matter is described as optional, while space, time, and energy are treated as mandatory. That leaves room for immaterial-but-energetic phenomena such as light, fields, or computational activity.

The phrase “exactly one frame of reference renders” requires careful interpretation. In ordinary physics, multiple observers can assign coordinates to the same event. A reference frame is ordinarily associated with an observer or coordinate system, not a finite private visual enclosure. [Einstein Online describes an observer/reference frame as assigning space and time coordinates to events](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/explandict/observer/).

The page becomes more coherent if `phenomenon` means not the shared physical event but a particular phenomenal token:

> Event (E) may be encountered by multiple agents, but each agent-frame produces its own rendered phenomenon (P_{a,n}(E)).

That preserves the “exactly one frame” rule without implying that only one observer can ever encounter an event.

## 5. The definition of noumenon and superposition

A noumenon is described as a superposition of a particular phenomenon, where the superposition represents multiple distinguishable configurations which cannot all be physically rendered inside one frame simultaneously.

Conceptually, this is a possibility-space interpretation:

[preformatted_text]

N_x = {x_1, x_2,...,x_k}

[end preformatted_text]

A frame renders one compatible configuration (x_i), while the noumenon encompasses the unresolved or unselected collection.

That is coherent as modal language. It is not yet quantum mechanics.

In quantum theory, a qubit superposition is not adequately represented by saying that a variable “is both zero and one.” It has complex amplitudes:

[preformatted_text]

∣ψ⟩ = α∣0⟩ + β∣1⟩, ∣α∣^2 + ∣β∣^2 = 1

[end preformatted_text]

Those amplitudes determine measurement statistics and interference behavior. A classical variable whose value is merely unspecified, or a set-valued variable (X\in{0,1}), is not automatically a quantum superposition. [IBM Quantum’s official learning material distinguishes classical bits, quantum states, superposition, and measurement](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/learning/courses/utility-scale-quantum-computing/bits-gates-and-circuits).

The page’s binary example is therefore useful as an intuitive picture of mutually exclusive alternatives, but insufficient as a technically quantum example.

## 6. Growing universes inside the static totality

The next proposition is the philosophical center of the page.

Within the static block multiverse called `nature` exist “growing block universes.” From inside one of those continua, a rendering frame appears to witness the accumulation of:

* Information.

* Temporal length.

* Sometimes spatial volume.

* Sometimes total energy.

The decisive word is `appears`.

That word allows the page to reconcile global stasis with local becoming. The encompassing block does not actually acquire new information. Instead, an internal agent experiences an increasingly long accessible prefix of a history.

This is not identical to the standard philosophical growing-block view, in which the past and present exist while the future literally does not yet exist. In the KARBYTES model, the future and its alternatives appear to be included already in the enclosing static totality. What grows is the agent-relative rendering or accessible prefix.

A more precise name for the resulting position would be:

> Static modal plenitude with agent-indexical becoming.

The claim that total energy can increase also needs qualification. In general relativity and expanding cosmology, global energy is not always uniquely definable or governed by an ordinary global conservation law; this depends on spacetime symmetries and the energy definition being used. A 2022 *Physical Review D* treatment explicitly discusses the relation between time-translation symmetry and cosmological energy conservation. [See Yu et al., “Correspondence between energy conservation and energy-momentum tensor conservation in cosmology.”](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevD.105.083511)

Consequently, “sometimes total energy” is not necessarily false, but the model would have to define:

* Which universe region is being measured.

* Which energy notion is used.

* Whether the region has a boundary or horizon.

* Which spacetime symmetries exist.

* Whether the claim concerns matter energy, vacuum energy, gravitational energy, or an effective cosmological quantity.

## 7. Frame of reference

The revised page defines a frame of reference as a finite region of apparent space that:

* Renders phenomena.

* Is substantiated by an information-processing agent.

* Is experienced by that agent.

* Appears preserved for a positive interval of time.

This is not the standard physics definition. It is a phenomenological-computational definition.

The phrase `substantiating and experiencing` is especially interesting. The agent is not pictured as a passive spectator located outside the frame. Its information processing helps constitute the experienced frame. The frame is both:

* The agent’s world-window.

* An output of the agent’s processing.

* The place in which the agent itself appears.

That is closer to an embodied rendering system, cognitive workspace, or conscious field than to an inertial coordinate system.

## 8. Meta-frame and frame

The definition then introduces two levels:

* A `frame of reference` is a meta-frame presenting the current experiential region.

* A `frame` is a finer unit of phenomenal rendering.

A frame has:

* Finite spatial dimensions.

* A relatively temporally atomic, perhaps timeless, temporal dimension.

* A unique bounded interval along a timeline.

This resembles animation or video:

* The timeline is an ordered sequence.

* Each frame is a bounded sample.

* The meta-frame is the experienced present assembled from one or more samples.

* Apparent motion emerges from ordered differences between frames.

There is, however, a small unresolved tension. A frame is described as possibly timeless while also being assigned a bounded temporal interval. A charitable resolution is that it is timeless internally but indexed externally:

[preformatted_text]

F_n ↔ [t_n,t_{n+1})

[end preformatted_text]

The frame itself contains no experienced internal passage; its interval specifies where it belongs in the timeline.

The word `unique` should refer to the frame-token’s position, not necessarily to its informational pattern. Two temporally separated frames could be informationally identical. Otherwise exact recurrence would be ruled out by definition.

## 9. Apparent travel through time

The page says that a frame of reference appears to its renderer to travel forward along the renderer’s timeline.

This resembles the moving-spotlight theory of time:

* The complete temporal structure exists.

* A privileged present appears to move along it.

But the page makes the spotlight agent-relative rather than cosmically universal. Different agents have different renderers and therefore different experiential presents.

In a strictly static ontology, nothing literally travels through the block. The apparent travel can instead be represented as successive indexing:

[preformatted_text]

R_a(0),R_a(1),R_a(2),...

[end preformatted_text]

where (R_a(n)) is agent (a)’s rendered present at index (n).

The feeling of movement arises because later frames contain memories or records representing earlier frames while not containing corresponding memories of later ones. That memory asymmetry is not yet specified on the page, but it would be one plausible mechanism for the experienced arrow of time.

## 10. Timeline

A timeline is defined as an apparent linear chronological succession of frames.

The definition is admirably concise, but it leaves several questions open:

* What determines which frame may follow another?

* Are timelines finite, infinite, cyclic, or branching?

* Can the same frame belong to several timelines?

* How are two agents’ timelines synchronized?

* What makes an agent at frame (n+1) numerically the same agent as at frame (n)?

* Can timelines merge?

* Are inconsistent histories excluded?

* Are causal loops allowed?

* Does every logically coherent timeline exist noumenally?

* What gives one rendered history its apparent probability rather than another?

Those questions do not invalidate the worldview. They identify the transition from ontology sketch to formal model.

The page’s hierarchy can be represented as follows:

[preformatted_text]

    B["Static block multiverse"] --> H["Complete universe histories"]
    H --> T["Agent-relative timelines"]
    T --> F["Present frames"]
    F --> P["Rendered phenomena"]
    N["Noumenal alternatives"] -->|rendering or selection| P

[end preformatted_text]

Let:

* (B) be the complete static block multiverse.

* (H) be a set of complete universe histories.

* (T_a) be the timeline experienced by agent (a).

* (F_a(n)) be agent (a)’s frame at position (n).

* (P_a(n)) be the finite phenomenal content rendered at that frame.

* (N_a(n)) be the larger set or state space of noumenal alternatives associated with it.

Then:

[preformatted_text]

T_a =⟨F_a(0),F_a(1),F_a(2),…⟩

[end preformatted_text]

and the agent’s accumulated experiential prefix is:

[preformatted_text]

G_a(n)=⟨F_a(0),F_a(1),…,F_a(n)⟩

[end preformatted_text]

The global object (B) never changes. But:

[preformatted_text]

G_a(n) ⊂ G_a(n+1)

[end preformatted_text]

so the internally available history appears to grow.

This formalization makes the central synthesis coherent:

* Global reality is static.

* Agent-relative history is prefix-expanding.

* Frames are finite.

* Phenomena are rendered contents.

* Noumena are unrendered alternatives or underlying state descriptions.

* Temporal passage is an indexical feature of an agent’s ordered experience.

# Relationship to established positions

The worldview combines elements of several positions without being reducible to any one of them.

## Eternalism or block-universe theory

It shares eternalism’s basic idea that the full spacetime history is complete rather than coming into existence moment by moment.

## Growing-block theory

It borrows the language of a growing past, but relocates growth inside an agent’s apparent history. Globally, the future is not genuinely absent.

## Moving spotlight

The frame of reference’s apparent forward travel resembles a spotlight moving across an already existent timeline.

## Modal realism

The encompassing domain of noumenal alternatives resembles a plenitude of possible configurations or histories.

## Everettian relative states

Its observer-relative rendering and superposition vocabulary have affinities with Everett, but Everett’s original formulation concerns states defined relative to interacting subsystems under quantum dynamics. It does not simply assert that every logically imaginable configuration exists. [Everett’s original 1957 relative-state paper is available through APS](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/RevModPhys.29.454).

## Kantian phenomena and noumena

The distinction between appearances and an unrendered domain is Kantian in ancestry, but KARBYTES gives `noumenon` a new computational and quantum-inflected meaning.

## Computationalism

The terminology of agents, processing, rendering, frames, information, and finite allocation makes this perhaps the page’s strongest affinity. Reality resembles a complete repository; an embodied agent behaves like a process rendering a restricted stream from it.

# Remaining conceptual pressure points

The page would need several additional definitions to become a formal theory:

1. A state space defining all permitted configurations.

2. A compatibility or transition rule between adjacent frames.

3. A rendering rule mapping noumena to phenomenal tokens.

4. A probability measure explaining why some histories are experienced.

5. A criterion of agent identity across frames.

6. A consistency relation between different agents’ renderings.

7. A definition of information independent of colloquial “amount of detail.”

8. A cosmological definition of total energy.

9. A distinction between classical possibility and quantum superposition.

10. At least one empirical consequence which differs from competing models.

Without those, the page remains an ontology rather than a scientific model. That is not a dismissal. Ontologies provide the categories within which later formal models can be formulated.
