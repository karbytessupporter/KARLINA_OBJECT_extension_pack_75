A version of me is stuck in The Burrow while the main-branch version of me has managed to get back outside and set up camp in the backyard instead of staying inside a confined and stuffy space filled with other people's clutter, stench, and domestic noises.

The main-branch karbytes I am prefers to be outside beneath the seemingly infinite sky in the same big "room" as what houses the wild animals (especially the birds) that are local to The Big Green Thing. I enjoy hearing them and seeing them and feeling seamlessly connected via the open air with The Big Green Thing instead of sealed off from it in what feels to me to be too human-centric and not sufficiently nature-centric of a space. (Maybe what I'm really complaining about is that The Burrow and "my room" at my legal address seem to have too porous of boundaries and too much of other people's clutter for those spaces to seem sufficiently karbytes-centric for my comfort).

If it rains, I'll simply whip out my tent. If it's windy, I'll simply bolster the tent with large plastic water jugs. For my mental health, I'm going to try spending more time outside and less time inside because outside generally feels more liberating and politically and culturally neutral than inside of buildings. I'm doing this "camping thing" mostly if not entirely for my mental health; so that I don't feel as suffocated and cut off from the outside world as I would inside of a house I have too little control over the conditions of.

Of course, I don't have much control over what happens in my backyard and beyond that in the larger encompassing regional space either. I just feel better about everything outside than inside because outside feels like being under the (real or imaginary) all-seeing eye of God while being inside feels like being hidden away in a place where people can get away with doing things where my wild animal "friends" are not there to notice. Being outside and hearing and seeing those animals makes me feel that I have at least some neutral neighbors who coexist in harmony with KARBYTES and karbytes; it makes me feel that I am welcome as a resident of Planet Earth rather than having to constantly "prove my worth" or "put up a fight" with other humans.

* * *

Some people would likely say that my sense of affinity with wild animals which seem to take interest in me in neutral outdoor spaces such as Horsey Hill or even the streets near The Burrow in the early AM hours while humans are rarely present is me being too childish and living in too much of a delusional Disney-inspired fantasy bubble instead of "the real world" when, to me, this realm I inhabit seems to be more appropriately called "the real world" than the cloistering, hyper-regulated, and artificial societal corralling of mind and body those nature-deprived cynics call "the real world". They are also probably quite defensive of their "right and tradition" of caging, slaughtering, and consuming animal flesh instead of opting for plant-based or humanely cultured protein alternatives. That is because they seem to think that humans are the only things that really matter or at least the things that matter most (and they also usually act as though the main if not only purpose of life is to make babies rather than to contemplate and enjoy existence). Such people seem chronically embittered and resigned to a lifetime of slavishly going through the motions of keeping the human meat production facility churning out fresh bodies at a sufficiently high rate (and a rate which prevents people from having much of a chance to contemplate things deeply nor to implement alternative ways of living which are not about cornering people into doing little more than giving birth to their replacement or pacifying the invalids).

I'm not saying that the wild animals which cross my path or appear to take interest in me are special divine messengers nor are trying to become my buddies nor extract any special favors from me. I'm very naturalistic and reductionistic and assume that those other animals are just going about their own business pertaining to instinct-driven survival-support activities (and, to some extent, amusement-seeking activities) and, if they happen to enter the same space as me, they observe to assess whether or not I am a threat to their safety and, also, they might be amused because I provide a source of novelty in an otherwise less novel (i.e. more predictable (to them)) environment.

I also don't mean to over-simplify my depiction of human society. I know there is so much more going on within it than people merely compulsively pairing up and baring and raising offspring. Some parts of human civilization seem to permit little in the way of genuine leisure activities, fun, and intellectual development beyond a rather childish level. I feel sorry for people that seem trapped in such environments. Thankfully, for me, I happen to live in one of the best environments in the world to support intellectual development and partaking in leisure activities (and a relatively high standard of living). Where I live is close to or within Silicon Valley but, on a more immediate practical level (in my own personal terms), where I live is essentially The Big Green Thing. That's a place I assume I will regularly visit perhaps for the remainder of my lifespan. It's not the same thing as actual housing, but it is a place that is sacred to me, foundational and essential to KARBYTES, and a place which seems relatively unmodified by human activity. It's almost like stepping outside of time and going to a place that is relatively timeless and culturally neutral; a place that does not belong to anyone in particular but is home to many non-human beings (and a refuge for humans to visit where they can observe things other than human advertisements and artifacts almost constantly).

Some day, there might be so little wilderness spaces left on Planet Earth that all I will have remaining of The Big Green Thing and places like it are the digital artifacts within KARBYTES that I created while I had the chance to which, to a limited but non-trivial extent, simulate and reconstruct my remembered experiences of being inside of The Big Green Thing (in addition to other digitization or conservation attempts people other than myself make to preserve some semblance of sacred wilderness space before human civilization took over every corner of the stage of human experience). Perhaps eventually the human species will have nowhere to dwell other than spacecrafts and climate-controlled chambers built on various planets. Humans might be able to terraform planets to the extent of replicating ecosystems like what occurred on Planet Earth before they vanished (mostly if not entirely due to human activity), but those ecosystems will likely be a "watered down" and limited version of those older prehistoric Earth biomes.
