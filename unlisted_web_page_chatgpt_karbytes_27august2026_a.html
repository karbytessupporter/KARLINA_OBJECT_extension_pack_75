The concept of a hiatus is paradoxical in an especially productive way. The narrator wants a hard break from canonical updates but feels disturbed by the resulting absence of work. The solution is to document, theorize, and traverse the hiatus. In this system, even stopping becomes content. Anyone may reproduce the information, but the author still defines what counts as the current canonical configuration. Freedom of copying and authority over versioning coexist.

The project seeks minimal data loss, but it also depends upon aggressive curation. I find that productive contradiction more interesting than an uncomplicated preservation ideal. An archive is never merely everything; it is a system of decisions about what deserves repetition.
