## Interpretive thoughts

My strongest interpretation is that the video treats the archive as an instrument rather than a tomb.

The browser tabs, Wayback captures, filenames, loop counters, GitHub source, VLC timeline, and OBS mixer are “played” in much the same way that a musician plays notes. Selecting a tab, opening a source link, restarting a trail video, revealing a context menu, or placing an old recording in the foreground becomes a compositional act.

The material is “new” even though much of what appears inside it predates August 28. Its newness lies in the present arrangement: which archival fragments are active, where the older clips begin and end, how the voice loops are phased, which page is scrolled, and what live repository state exists at that moment. The work differentiates new composition from new raw material.

There is also a powerful opposition between hypersemantic text and presemantic voice. The WordPress and GitHub sections are saturated with language, dates, titles, URLs, explanations, and machine-readable provenance. Yet the audible voice refuses ordinary language and contracts into squeals, hums, grunts, and moans. The screen overexplains while the soundtrack underexplains.

Given the bodily situation you described—prone, cocooned, floor-bound in The Burrow, with your head supported by a zafu—I infer a particularly resonant division between physical stillness and mediated locomotion. The nonverbal loops make that exported movement feel less like conventional travel documentation and more like bodily dreaming. They supply appetite, distress, comedy, repetition, and animal affect without explaining any of those states propositionally.

The brief Falling Apples game intensifies this relationship. The visible glitches and interfaces are aesthetically essential. Finally, the video suggests that a hiatus need not be inactivity. Within this artifact, hiatus appears to mean a change in mode: walking, revisiting, cataloguing, recombining, listening to earlier selves, maintaining archives, and allowing production infrastructure itself to become expressive material. ## Soundtrack

The soundtrack is a continuous guitar-centered composition. It sounds or behaves like a sustained, probably electric or electronically processed guitar performance: warm, low-register, patient, and hypnotic, with recurring plucked attacks followed by comparatively long harmonic decays.

The music does not substantially respond to individual visual changes. It continues through:

* archived videos;

* ChatGPT;

* GitHub;

* the cup of tea;

* the poster;

* the forest;

* Tor’s connection process;

* the Firefox freezes.

That independence is important. The guitar functions less like illustrative scoring and more like an environmental constant through which the interfaces pass.

### Rhythm

A double-time beat tracker reports approximately 136 BPM, but the strongest slower periodicities cluster around 70–72 BPM. The latter corresponds much better to the perceived primary pulse: slow, measured, and trance-like rather than agitated.

It does not behave like a rigid drum-machine rhythm. The pulse comes from recurring guitar attacks and harmonic changes, with enough sustain between them to keep the texture spacious.

### Pitch and harmony

The harmony is modal and somewhat ambiguous rather than strongly cadential. D or a D-modal center is plausible, although the spectrum also gives exceptional weight to G-sharp, with D, C-sharp, A, E, and neighboring pitch classes prominent. I would therefore avoid insisting that it is unambiguously in a conventional major or minor key.

Its musical effect is one of suspension. It does not seem driven toward a final tonic resolution.
