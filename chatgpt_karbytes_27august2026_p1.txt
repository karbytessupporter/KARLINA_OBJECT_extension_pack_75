### Detailed thematic description

The monologue progresses through five conceptual layers.

#### 1. Immediate ecological event and administrative situation

A dragonfly eating a gnat is the opening observation. The speaker then describes having uploaded the afternoon’s videos to the staging repository.

The movement is strikingly rapid:

> predation in the sky → files in GitHub → canon update → ChatGPT traversal

A microscopic local event is immediately followed by an abstract archival system.

#### 2. What KARBYTES is

The speaker attempts several institutional classifications:

* one-person organization;

* quasi-business;

* private church;

* personal religion;

* institution;

* fantasy/reality hybrid;

* cyberspace world.

None is accepted as sufficient. The repeated inability to classify KARBYTES becomes part of the definition. It crosses boundaries among artwork, autobiography, infrastructure, philosophy, software, religion, institution, and world simulation.

#### 3. Computation, mathematics, and physics

The university experience is described as incomplete institutionally but formative intellectually. Computer science supplies an algorithmic worldview. Mathematics is treated as an abstract domain of numbers and relationships. Implemented computation introduces physical limitations such as memory and energy.

The speaker’s disciplinary hierarchy is informal rather than technically rigorous—computer science is not simply mathematics “using physics,” because theoretical computer science can remain abstract—but the intended distinction is intelligible:

* mathematics studies abstract structure;

* computation studies transformations and procedures;

* physical implementation introduces finite resources and energetic cost.

KARBYTES tries to connect all three.

#### 4. Personal sovereignty and other people

The archive is explicitly centered on one person’s perspective and interests. Yet the public cyberspace end product is imagined as an interface through which other people can enter that perspective.

This modifies the one-person-universe concept. Other people are not coauthors or central characters, but they are anticipated as visitors, interpreters, and future interactants.

#### 5. Infinite growth versus completion

The speaker desires infinite expansion but also feels trapped by the production process. A break requires writing about a break, which creates more content and postpones the break.

The preservation pipeline—GitHub staging, canon integration, ChatGPT processing, Wayback captures, M-DISCs—allows the archive to continue, but every preservation act is additional work.

The final ten-year rule provides an external stopping condition. Absolute finalization is not declared merely because karbytes feels temporarily finished. It occurs after an extremely long absence of further updates.
